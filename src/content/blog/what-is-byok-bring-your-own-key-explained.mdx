# What is BYOK AI? Bring Your Own Key Explained

If you have shopped for AI software lately, you have seen "BYOK" — bring your own key. It changes who pays for AI usage, who sees your data, and what the software costs you monthly. Here is how it works.

## The two pricing models

**Proxied access (the default SaaS model).** The vendor holds an account with an AI provider, and your requests flow through their infrastructure. You never see the provider's bill; instead you pay a subscription that bundles usage with margin. Simple to start, but every AI feature is rented at a markup forever.

**BYOK (bring your own key).** You obtain an API key directly from the AI provider and paste it into the software. Your requests go from the software straight to the provider, billed to *your* account at raw rates. The vendor never sits in the middle of your AI traffic.

## Why BYOK usually costs less

LLM providers charge per token. A drafting-heavy month might cost a few dollars of raw API usage. Under a proxied model, the same volume arrives wrapped inside a $29–$99 subscription — because the vendor is reselling tokens plus margin.

With BYOK:

- **You pay wholesale prices** directly to the provider
- **Heavy usage scales linearly** — no tier upgrades because you posted more
- **Light usage costs almost nothing** — a quiet month bills pennies
- **You can switch providers** by swapping one key, not changing products

The software vendor charges for the software (or gives it away, if open source) instead of taxing your token consumption.

## The security picture

BYOK done right is safer than handing credentials to every SaaS you try:

- **Keys stay yours.** Revoke one key and access ends everywhere it was used.
- **Scoped permissions.** Provider dashboards let you restrict keys to specific models or spend caps.
- **Encryption at rest matters.** Good BYOK software encrypts your key server-side before storing it — Joey uses AES-256-GCM, so a database leak does not leak your provider keys.

Two rules: never paste production keys into tools that cannot explain where they are stored, and rotate keys periodically.

## What BYOK asks of you

Honest trade-offs:

1. **One more account.** You need an API key from an LLM provider (and in Joey's case, a [Zernio](https://zernio.com) key for publishing).
2. **Usage visibility.** You will see the provider bill directly — some people find this a feature, others a chore.
3. **Self-serve support.** If your key hits rate limits, you resolve it with the provider, not the software vendor.

For most individuals and small teams these are minor. For anyone posting at volume, the savings overwhelm the setup cost within weeks.

## BYOK + open source: the full stack

BYOK pairs naturally with open source. Joey combines both: MIT-licensed code you can self-host and audit, your own LLM key for generation, and your own Zernio key for cross-platform publishing. No subscription gates features, and no middleman marks up your AI spend.
