# Open-Source Social Media Management: Joey vs Buffer vs Hootsuite

Buffer and Hootsuite defined social media management for a decade. In 2026 a third category has matured: open-source, AI-native tools you can self-host. This comparison covers where each approach wins, with [Joey](/) as the open-source representative.

## The quick version

| Dimension | Joey (open source) | Buffer | Hootsuite |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| License | MIT, self-hostable | Proprietary SaaS | Proprietary SaaS |
| Pricing model | Free + your own API keys | Subscription tiers | Subscription tiers |
| Content creation | AI agent drafts per platform | AI assist add-on | AI assist add-on |
| Approval workflow | Built-in human-in-the-loop queue | Basic approval flow | Team approval flows |
| Data ownership | Your database entirely | Vendor's cloud | Vendor's cloud |
| Customization | Full source access | None | None |

## Pricing: subscription margin vs raw cost

Buffer's free tier covers a handful of channels; real usage lands on paid plans. Hootsuite sits at the higher end, historically around $99/month for professional use.

The open-source math is different. Joey is free software — the costs are your LLM API usage and a publishing-layer key, both at provider rates. With BYOK there is no per-seat or per-channel markup, so heavy publishers often save hundreds of dollars monthly versus SaaS seats. The trade-off: you (or your team) operate the deployment.

## Automation depth: scheduler vs agent

This is the sharpest difference.

**Schedulers** excel at delivery. You compose posts in their composer, attach media, and pick time slots. Their AI features summarize text or suggest tweaks inside that composer. A human writes everything; the tool ships it.

**An agent like Joey inverts the flow.** You configure brand voice, platforms, and a posting schedule once. The agent then generates drafts on schedule — platform-specific formatting included — and queues them for review. Your weekly job drops from "write five posts" to "approve five drafts." For content-heavy teams this is hours back per week, not minutes.

Neither model is universally better. If writing is your core competency and you only want logistics handled, a scheduler fits. If consistent presence matters more than authorship control down to the comma, an agent wins.

## Trust and control: who can see your data

SaaS tools hold your account credentials, content history, and analytics on their infrastructure, governed by their security practices. That is fine for most teams and it is the zero-ops option.

Self-hosted Joey keeps credentials in your database with keys encrypted at rest (AES-256-GCM), behind auth you control. Auditing what software does with your social accounts becomes reading source code instead of trusting policy documents. Regulated industries and privacy-conscious brands weigh this heavily.

## Where Buffer and Hootsuite still win

Honest assessment:

- **Zero maintenance.** SaaS means someone else patches the servers.
- **Mature integrations.** Decades of network partnerships mean broad platform coverage.
- **Team features at scale.** Enterprise permissioning and client management are polished.
- **Support contracts.** A phone number to call when something breaks.

Open source trades those for transparency, cost control, and modern automation depth.

## When Joey is the right choice

Pick an open-source AI agent if you:

- Want drafts generated *for* you under human approval, not just scheduled
- Prefer paying raw API costs over subscription margins
- Need data residency and auditable code
- Are comfortable running a container or small server
